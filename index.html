Forecasting Methodology, Metrics & Limitations

How AQI Forecast models air quality and how to interpret uncertainty

1. WAQI Ground-Station AQI & Open-Meteo Weather

Live AQI, pollutant readings, rolling station history, and multi-day pollutant forecasts come directly from physical CPCB / state-board ground monitoring stations via the WAQI (World Air Quality Index) API. Weather variables (temperature, humidity, wind, rain) and Planetary Boundary Layer Height (PBLH) come from the Open-Meteo ECMWF Weather API.

2. Indian NAQI vs. US AQI & GRAP Stages

Toggle between India's CPCB National AQI (0–50 Good, 51–100 Satisfactory, 101–200 Moderate, 201–300 Poor, 301–400 Very Poor, 401–500 Severe) and the US EPA AQI scale. For Delhi-NCR cities, the GRAP banner maps Indian AQI to CAQM's Graded Response Action Plan thresholds (Stages I–IV).

3. WAQI-Powered Dispersion & Forecast Modeling

Inspired by operational systems like IITM AIRWISE, the 12-hour and 72-hour forecasts are modeled directly from WAQI ground-station history and WAQI's daily PM2.5/PM10 forecast, shaped hourly using ridge-regularized regression over meteorology, boundary-layer ventilation (Ventilation Index = Wind × PBLH), wind direction, and rain washout.

4. Key Limitations & Responsible Use

  • Grid vs. Street-Level Sensors: Forecast grid cells represent multi-kilometer averages, not individual roadside monitors.
  • AOD vs. Surface PM2.5: Aerosol Optical Depth measures column-integrated haze through the full atmosphere and is not identical to breathing-level PM2.5.
  • Sudden Spikes & Fires: Episodic agricultural burning, fireworks, or local construction spikes cannot be fully predicted before they occur.
  • What-If Emission Scenarios: A 20% or 40% local emissions cut does not reduce ambient AQI by 20% or 40% due to upwind regional transport and secondary chemistry.
  • Official Decisions: Pair forecast trends with official CPCB, CAQM, and IITM AIRWISE bulletins for operational or health-critical decisions.
